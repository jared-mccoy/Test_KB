Does artificial [[666dd88711140132dd987948|artificial intelligence]] not beckon us like a [[672134409085efba34317792|ghost]]? Why do we draw back? Is it because we fear that what should only emerge from the interiority of a real, human [[6721851f22e8943c62038166|depth]], might issue from a spectral presence without a name? What if one day we could no longer be made to read or write? What if it became impossible to tell if someone actually read what is written about or wrote what appears beneath the [[672134403f160593a219901f|signature]] of their name?[^1]  We might prepare for a future in which this [[666b8f033e9e05fba8e92917|event]] would not obliterate the very possibility of [[67213440f7cd3654c5ded7ec|scholarship]] in the humanities or [[66fc81796d3913af5e94692a|humanity]] as such. Is it possible to face this spectre without seeing ourselves defaced?[^2] To foresee a future wherein the singularity of the [[67213440922a15604bd4aca5|human]] is not undone by that of the [[672134402fd8a7ec1c4d1296|technological]]? 

Art thou a scholar?[^3]

[^2]: Cf. [[67213440afa1c199985188e6|Autobiography as De-facement]] and other instances of this theme in de Man's works.
