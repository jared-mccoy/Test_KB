**Problem:** Educators struggle to visualize complex, foundational ideas, while flashy visuals oversimplify important content. Explainer videos prioritize superficial engagement, misrepresenting learning as passive consumption instead of active engagement with challenging topics.

**Examples:**

- **Technology:** Distributed systems are reduced to basic diagrams, ignoring core challenges like consensus protocols, fault tolerance, and scaling—key elements needed to understand cloud infrastructure.
    
- **Science:** Genetic inheritance is often reduced to basic DNA diagrams and Punnett squares, overlooking gene expression, epigenetics, and polygenic traits—essential for understanding how environmental factors impact gene activation.
    
- **Law/Politics:** Recent Supreme Court rulings, such as those on voting rights, are often reduced to static summaries, missing the complex judicial reasoning, dissenting opinions, and political stakes that shape these outcomes.
    
- **Literature:** Structural analyses of narrative techniques and intertextuality are reduced to basic summaries, missing the comprehensive understanding needed for literary works and their cultural significance.
    

We need tools that balance clarity, depth, and accessibility.

**Objective:** To develop an innovative visualization solution that addresses the disconnect between overly reductive visuals and the need for intellectually rigorous content. This solution aims to communicate complex ideas in a way that is both intellectually rigorous and widely accessible, enabling educators to convey complex knowledge effectively.

nodeIdiate: The former, aesthetic fork of this project, focused on conceptualizing and researching tools and techniques for a new form of animated content called "ideation" or "eidetic diagramming." Developed a unique style of animation to effectively convey the formation and disintegration of complex ideas, offering a richer and more fluid alternative to traditional explainer videos. reticulateKb: The latter, infrastrucural, part of the project involved the migration of a SQL database of some 50k citations amassed over the course of my graduate research, to a new database capable of managing complex relationships involving full text and media documents and the multittude of instances / states these might assume throughout their history as drafts, revisions, citations , etc. vis a vis one another. The platform I had been using, Citavi, which served me thoughout my graduate studies and of which i have written about elsewhere, excellent for basic archival needs, was not very well equipped for the task at hand, generating a comprehensive network map of ones knowledgebase , not just to look at and admire, but to meaninfully interact with as a means of navigating the history of ones work and ideas.

Producing / Refining (2023-12-2024-4) 

Hegel's Phenomenology: Out of the countless thinkers I could have chosen to test nodeIdeates's ability to express complexity of thought in motion I knew that there was one capable of providing the absolute test. GWF Hegel. I chose him because he is usually the thinker that makes Philosophy students consider changing majors or dropping out. Moreover I wanted something that infamously challenging, and alientingly abstract. Eventually I settled of the first chapter of his phenomenology, Sensuous certainty as my test subject with the rationale that: if it works here it works anywhere. Hegel also seemed a good place to test out my own triadic approach to representing complexity (Reading , Analysis, Interpretation) which prioritize a raw unfiltered encounter with the text, a analytical mapping intended to be as objective / unbiased as possible, and an interpretation, in which we inflect and interpret the meaning of the structure.   
  
Human / Nature / Technics: With nodeIdeate up an running I was finally able to complete what ended up being the 3rd version of a longform introductory lecture modeled of a non-fiction science course that I had taught numerous times over the years. Unlike the Hegel series, my intention here was to attempt and introductory style lecture that is closer in its form and content to the popular explainer, while using the affordances of the medium to avoid common pitfalls. Essentially the idea was to start with the familiar, superficial topics one finds in explainers and, without forcing it, grow these organically into a nuanced and critical overview of the topic providing an introduction to an undergraduate class that is accessible to students and non-specialists without letting the content devolve into mere facts, anecdotes or summary, building an actual framework for stustained inquiry and analysis.   
  
Surface / Scholar: Supplementing the (deliberately) minimal aesthetic I used for some of the analytical topics I wanted this series to be a kind of mission statement for the brand as a whole (a profession de foi). Here i strove to intersperse the intensive concept mappings with more poetic / lyrical expression of text and image showing how the conceptual and analytical diagram supplement and enhance other modes of knowledge visualiztion.   
  
  
  
Phase 2: reticulateKb

Research:I knew that I was looking for something like neo4j, or Gephi , but something that also fulfilled the needs of a editor , note taking, reference manager that could fulfill the needs of whatever kind of document one might throw at it from personal notes to the most formal academic / professional writing. Here Obsidian became a frontrunner as a markdown native knowledge managers with the ability to, navigate and visualize inbound/outbound document links (essential for managing citations) and the ability  instantly publish ones knowledebase (or selections thereof) instantly serving as a static site generator (with the paid services). Combined with the ability metadata via YAML frontmatter and its growing popularity this made it an easy choice over other KMS. 

Limitations : The main question though, when thinking about migrating from a traditional SQL db to a md based system (which doesn't expose its own db methods via api) is how to leverage the incredibly versatile and fast front Obsidean front end without losing ones ability to perform comprehensive, precise etl operations you get with a traditional DBMS. Esseentially, as great as obsidian may be when it comes to managing / visualizing the discrete relatiionships between knowledge items, I can't really justify just discarding a fully normalized database for a bunch of md files with YAML. there needs to be a middle path, and this, in effect was the inception of kbMgr: a robust CLI capable of managing multiple DB instances and outputs at once, while streamlining db ops between various SQL and NOSQL implementations and front end KMS like Obsidian, neo4j, Gephi etc. Essentially the goal is to polyglot persistence between your primary db and your visualizers KMS and, in a manner that allows for seamless integration of DB , editor visualizer and web endpoint. I.e. the database: is the graph : is the site. while so far I had only really scratched the surface of js building nodeIdeate, I felt like I was ready to take on this new task head on
