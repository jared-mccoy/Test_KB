Yes, there is a literal connection between the Greek word **δαίμων (daimon)** and the modern word "demon," though the meanings have diverged significantly over time.

### Original Meaning in Greek:

- **Δαίμων (daimon)** in ancient Greek referred to a spirit or divine power, which could be either good or bad. It was a neutral term and often described a guiding or protective spirit associated with a person or place. **Daimons** were considered intermediaries between gods and humans, and their influence could be beneficial, protective, or sometimes harmful, but they were not inherently evil.

### Evolution into "Demon":

- Over time, particularly with the rise of Christianity, the term **daimon** began to take on a more negative connotation. In Christian theology, **δαίμονες (daimones)** became associated with malevolent spirits or fallen angels, which is how the term "demon" came to be understood in its modern sense.
    
- The transformation of **daimon** into "demon" involved a shift from the idea of a morally neutral or ambivalent spirit to one that is inherently evil. This shift was influenced by the Christian demonization of pagan deities and spirits, which were recast as demons opposed to the Christian God.
    

### Summary:

The connection between **δαίμων** and "demon" is indeed real, but they are not false friends (**faux amis**). Rather, they are related words whose meanings have diverged over time due to cultural and religious shifts. **Δαίμων** in Greek originally referred to a spirit or divine power, while "demon" in modern usage specifically refers to an evil supernatural being.
